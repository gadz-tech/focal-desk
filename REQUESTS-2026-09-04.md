## §1 — Focus comes from the tab only. Never from a side-panel interaction.

> "tabs work great.... when they work. they always do manual refocusing but
> sometimes the focus comes from interacting with a side panel (exactly what i
> dont want anymore)"

- With `dwell_ms = 0` a click, keystroke, scroll or selection **inside a side
  window's body must never promote it**. Today it sometimes does. Find the path:
  the likeliest is the OS foreground change (the WinEvent hook / rescan seeing a
  new foreground window) being turned into a promotion, or a `Promoted` sent from
  somewhere other than the tab tap / hotkey / focal-slot drop. Whatever it is,
  the engine gets an invariant: **the only sources of `Promoted` are the tab tap,
  the hotkey and the drop on the focal slot**; an OS foreground change is
  informational (it may update "active" styling) and never re-places anything.
- Tests pin it: a foreground change to a side window with dwell off → zero
  `Place` commands; a body click → zero; a tab tap → exactly the promote.
- Log every promotion with its source (`tab` / `hotkey` / `drop`) so the next
  stray one names itself in `focal-desk.log`.

## §2 — Copy in one side window, paste in any other. Nothing moves.

> "i need to be able to copy and paste in one window on the side to any other
> window. right now it still sometimes switches and the copy breaks and gets
> lost."

- Same root as §1, but it has its own acceptance test because it is the thing
  Ryan does all day: select text in side window A, Ctrl+C, click into side
  window B, Ctrl+V — **no window is re-placed, the selection survives, the paste
  lands**. Ten times in a row, across at least three app pairs (a terminal, a
  browser, Claude Desktop). Also: a drag-select that crosses the frame must not
  become a tab drag (the tab drag starts only from a pointer-down *on the frame*).
- If anything in the adapter re-asserts placement on a foreground change or on a
  timer (the 120 Hz loop re-placing "drifted" windows), it must not fire while the
  pointer button is down or within ~500 ms of a keystroke in that window — or,
  simpler, not fire at all on foreground changes (§1).

## §3 — The frame: encapsulate the whole window. Frosted glass, rounded, thick toward center.

> "for the tabs, encapsulating the whole window. frosted glass rounded edges,
> windows vista but updated to modern times with a hint of neon 1980s. for where
> the connection cables are going to go. Slim on all sides but the side facing
> the center will still be thick. just like it is now."

## §4 — Frames sit BEHIND their window, and under anything fullscreen.

> "tabs should not be over everything else, they should actually be behind thier
> parent window. I was watching youtube last night and the tabs were still there,
> along with claude deskop on top of the video."

- **No `WS_EX_TOPMOST` on the frames.** Z-order: a frame sits *immediately below*
  its window (`SetWindowPos(frame, hwndInsertAfter = window)` after every z-order
  change the hook reports — foreground, reorder, minimize/restore). The window
  covers the frame's inner region; the visible part is the ring outside. A frame
  can never be above any window that is above its own window.
- **Fullscreen**: when the foreground window covers the whole monitor (a video,
  a game, a presentation), all frames hide until it stops. Today's "hide while
  suspended or inactive" stays.
- A frame whose window is minimized, hidden or off-monitor hides too.

## §8 — Assignable slots: per-app slot preferences, Ryan edits them himself.

> "assignable slots. i want a feature where something like my household screen
> always gets priority there. terminals will priority to the corners, browsers
> usually to the sides. mail to the top or bottom. i want to be able to assign
> all these myself."

- **Config**, in the conf beside the exe, plain text Ryan edits: an ordered list
  of rules, each `match` (exe name, window class, or a title substring — first
  hit wins) → `slots = [..]` (ordered preference, slot names as `layout` knows
  them: corners, sides, top, bottom, the focal slot) and `priority = true|false`.
  Example shape (F picks the exact keys):

      [[rule]]  match = "household"    slots = ["top"]                 priority = true
      [[rule]]  match = "WindowsTerminal.exe"  slots = ["top-left","top-right","bottom-left","bottom-right"]
      [[rule]]  match = "chrome.exe"   slots = ["left","right"]
      [[rule]]  match = "olk.exe"      slots = ["top","bottom"]

- **Engine** (`focal-core`, no OS calls): placement consults the rules. A new
  window takes the first *free* slot in its list; none free → today's fallback.
  `priority = true` means it **claims** its first slot even if occupied — the
  occupant moves to its own next preference (or the fallback). No rule → today's
  behavior. `MoveTo` by hand always wins over a rule for that window until it
  closes.
- Tests pin: rule → first free preferred slot; priority claim moves the occupant
  to its next preference; a hand `MoveTo` is not overridden by a later rescan;
  no rule → unchanged placement; a bad rule line is logged and skipped, never a
  crash.
- Later (not today): "remember this" — a hotkey while dragging that writes the
  rule for you.

## §9 — Never resize a window while it flies. Fly a snapshot; resize once, at the end.

> "for performance, we shouldnt try to resize the windows while they move. For
> some things it works. for fusion or kicad it sometimes breaks the application.
> screen grab low res, scale that, refresh at new size." (Ryan, 12:20)

- The fly animation today re-sizes the real window every frame; Fusion and KiCad
  sometimes break under it (their GPU surfaces re-create on every size change).
- New: at the start of a move, **grab the window once at low resolution**
  (`PrintWindow` / DWM thumbnail — `DwmRegisterThumbnail` is the cheapest: no
  copy at all, scaled by DWM), hide or leave the real window where it is, and
  animate the **snapshot** — scaled — along the path. At the destination: one
  `SetWindowPos` with the final rect, then drop the snapshot when the window has
  painted at its new size (a short grace, ~2 frames, or `WM_PAINT`/DWM
  frame-done). One resize per move, ever.
- Apps that were fine being resized live gain nothing but lose nothing; the rule
  is uniform, no per-app list.
- Tests pin (engine side): a `MoveTo` emits exactly one final `Place`; the
  animation is the adapter's business and never sends intermediate placements
  through the engine.
